How to learn to sew from scratch: the order that works

What to buy to start, the hand stitches you need, when to move to the machine and the first projects that turn out well.

A starter kit

Hand needles in a few sizes, neutral polyester thread, pins, scissors used only for fabric, a seam ripper, a tape measure and chalk or a washable marker. To practise, scraps of non-stretch cotton: the most forgiving fabric there is.

By hand first

Before the machine, five stitches: tacking, backstitch, overcasting, blanket stitch and ladder (invisible) stitch. With them you can already take up a hem, close a split seam and sew on a button that stays on.

Then the machine

Threading it, sewing straight along a line, understanding tension and stitch length, backstitching, corners and curves. Practise on paper with no thread, following lines, before moving to fabric.

Mending before garments

Shortening trousers, darning, patching or replacing a zip saves money and shows you how clothes are made. Patterns come next: measuring yourself, choosing a size, tracing, darts, gathers and a toile in cheap fabric.

First projects

A lavender sachet, napkins, a cushion cover and a tote bag. They are straight, forgive mistakes and get used every day, which is what keeps you going.
